# ADVERSARIAL PROCEDURAL ROUTING AUDIT

Generated by this directory's own loop: `gen_problems.py` invents the problems, `run_ladder.py` makes the Rust bus router solve them and proves each solution in the vanilla-accurate engine, `verify.py` tries to beat the solution using only the solver's own knobs, and `critic.py` asks codex (gpt-5.6-sol) to find something better — with every claim it makes mechanically checked before it counts.

```
python3 gen_problems.py --out problems --mode both --per-tier 6 --seed 20260810
python3 run_ladder.py  --problems problems --out results.jsonl --work-dir work
python3 verify.py      --results results.jsonl --out variants.jsonl
python3 critic.py      --results results.jsonl --out critiques.jsonl \
                       --log codex_log.jsonl --max-calls 60
python3 make_report.py
```

## 3b. Demolition evidence: how much of the refresh is load-bearing?

`prune_check.py` takes the router's own exported schematic, deletes repeaters until each lane's refresh pitch is >= 15 (dust reaches 15 cells), and re-runs every vector through mc-tick over an INDEPENDENT Python path (`rs.py` conventions: lever toggling, lamp reads). The baseline is proved first, so a failure cannot be blamed on the harness.

* **54 problems: the wider pitch still delivers every word** — 947 of 2470 repeaters (38%) were provably redundant.
* **9 problems: the dense pitch is load-bearing** — widening it breaks most vectors. These are the interesting ones: p_dy_5, p_form_2, p_form_4, p_gates_1, t3_01, t3_02, t3_03, t3_04, t3_05.
* 24 skipped or not applicable (multi-bus/fanout banks cannot be attributed by scanning, or the pitch was already >= 15).

So the refresh policy is not simply wrong — it is *unconditional*. On straight and lightly-obstructed runs it is roughly twice as dense as the physics needs; on form conversions and level shifts the density is exactly what keeps the word alive. That is a per-segment decision the router currently does not make.

Read the `replies with NO claims` column before the `accepted/call` column. At high effort 3 of 5 replies contained no checkable claim at all (two of them answered with a bare verdict word that is not even in the schema), so the high row's ratio rests on very few replies. Cost per usable claim runs the other way: 23,335 tokens per claim at low vs 130,395 at high.

**Verdict on the tiering: low effort was the right default.** It produced the most claims per token, it produced the single most valuable idea in this whole report (the refresh-pitch critique, from the very first low-effort call), and the medium pass — run only where low found nothing — did not rescue those problems: it mostly re-stated them. High effort did not find a class of problem the cheap pass had missed; it mostly wrote less.

## 7. Was codex actually useful as a critic? — measured, not assumed

* 54 calls, 53 usable replies, 2,060,316 tokens.
* 32 claims survived verification. Of those, **11 were things the codex-free variant sweep had already found by itself** — codex agreed with a machine that costs nothing to run, and 3 were plain measurements of the existing route.
* **18 rest on the ONE idea the sweep could not reach**: that the refresh pitch is unconditional. That idea came from the very first low-effort call, it required building a whole new verifier (`prune_check.py`) to test, and it turned out to be right on simple runs and wrong on form conversions — a distinction worth more than the original claim.
* 24 claims could not be checked at all, mostly because the solver exposes no knob for what codex proposed. That is the honest ceiling of this design: a critic can only be graded on the axes the system under test lets you actuate.

So: **useful, but not in the way the design assumed.** It did not act as a better router — none of its geometric counter-proposals for a specific route beat the solver when executed. It acted as a POLICY reviewer: it noticed a standing rule that was too conservative, which no amount of per-problem search would have surfaced, because every variant in the search inherits that same rule. If this loop is kept, spend the budget on breadth at low effort and invest the saved tokens in more verifier CHANNELS (things like `prune_check.py`), because the verifier's reach — not the critic's intelligence — was the binding constraint throughout.

## 8. Appendix: exactly where finding #3 lives

The refresh policy is one constant:

```
src/design.rs:131          const REFRESH_AT: u32 = 7;
src/design_promote.rs:99   const REFRESH_AT: usize = 6;
```

Seven dust cells between refreshes is a repeater every ~8 cells, which is what the fragments measure. Dust carries 15. The measurements in section 3b say the margin is real but *unconditionally* spent:

* on straight and lightly-obstructed runs, deleting every repeater closer than 15 to its neighbour still delivers every word — so the cost and half the delay are being paid for nothing;
* on form conversions and level shifts the same widening breaks the word, because turns, steps and dips spend strength that a flat cell does not.

So the fix is not "raise the constant": it is to make the refresh decision from the strength actually spent along the segment being emitted, with the conservative value kept as the fallback for segment kinds whose budget is not modelled. Anyone doing that work has a ready-made regression set — these problems MUST stay dense, because widening them is empirically fatal:

* `p_dy_5` (w8-s32-vert2vert-dy8-dl0-n1-none-g0-f1-binary) — pruning to pitch 15 breaks 16 of 18 vectors.
